## The single proposed change

Add one private bulk helper and one `Lookup` dispatch: for AVX2-capable compiled paths and n>=128, use a fixed64-entry queue of **uint32 bucket indices**; otherwise execute the original loop. Depth64 and threshold128 are chosen once from the already observed public Boost pipeline, not a sweep. No byte-size threshold, tuning knob or adaptive fallback is proposed.

The queue occupies256 source-level stack bytes, with no heap allocation, object field or persistent scratch. Fill it with `Block(hashes[0..63])`, issuing a read/T0 prefetch for each valid bucket. For i from0 to n-65, save queue[i&63], replace it with `Block(hashes[i+64])`, prefetch that future bucket, then query the saved bucket using the original `Mask(hashes[i])` and load/test expression and write out[i]. Drain the final64 saved indices in order. Keep masks at consumption; do not store64 SIMD masks. This computes every range address once but reads each input hash once for address preparation and once for mask consumption. The extra sequential hash reads, queue traffic and actual stack frame are costs to measure, not free work.

`ContainsHash`, `LookupOne`, `Mask`, `Block`, insertion, constructor, allocation, physical geometry and scalar fallback stay byte-identical. The private prepared-bucket test duplicates the exact original load/test expression rather than refactoring the visible singleton path. No force-inline/noinline hints, new ISA requirement, parallel wrapper or common-hash change. Public output order and count remain exact. Every prefetched address comes from an in-range bucket; n>=128 makes n-64 safe, and the steady guard makes i+64<n. Null+n0 stays on the original path. Repeated buckets and the two buckets sharing one64-byte line are valid.

The fixed dispatch preserves b1 and b31 source paths. Their generated code/layout can still change; preserve actual ASM and measure them as controls. A pipeline failure must not trigger an unplanned distance, threshold, mask or allocation change.
